Accurate, honest information about Odia typing requirements for Odisha government jobs — verified directly from official OSSC and OSSSC notifications. We correct common myths that mislead students.

Always verify from the official notification before your exam. Common myth to avoid: Many websites say "OSSC requires 30 WPM for LDC." This is wrong for Odisha — OSSC does not conduct LDC recruitment. That 30 WPM figure applies to SSC CHSL (Central Government), not OSSC (Odisha State). Always check whether the job is under central or state government.

Important: Which Keyboard Layout Does the Exam Use?

The OSSC typing test (Junior Grade Typist 2024-25) uses the Odia Inscript keyboard layout — not the Akruti English Phonetic layout that this website teaches. This is an important difference. Check your specific notification carefully.

Here is the difference between the two layouts you will encounter in Odisha:

Two Odia Keyboard Layouts
LayoutUsed InHow It Works
Akruti English Phonetic
(What this site teaches)		 DTP centres, newspapers, private offices, some older govt offices English letters mapped phonetically to Odia — k = କ, g = ଗ. f = Halant
Odia Inscript
(OSSC exam 2024-25)		 Government exam centres, Unicode standard Keys mapped to Odia based on standard Inscript layout — different key positions from Akruti

Why practice Akruti on this site then? Akruti is the dominant layout in Odisha's private sector — DTP, newspapers, publishing, and many offices. Learning it builds strong Odia typing fundamentals — speed, accuracy, finger placement — that transfer to any Odia layout. If your exam specifies Inscript, you should also practice on Inscript-specific tools. Our site gives you the typing discipline; the layout can be adapted.

How to Find Your Exact Requirements

Before starting preparation, do these 3 steps:

  1. Download the official notification PDF from ossc.gov.in or osssc.gov.in for your specific post.
  2. Find the "Typing Skill Test" section — note the exact WPM, keyboard layout, test duration, and error rules stated there.
  3. Practice on the exact layout and software specified in your notification before the exam date.

Useful tip: In the OSSC 2024-25 typing test, the Odia typing test carries 50 marks added to your final merit score — it is not just qualifying. This means higher speed and accuracy directly improves your rank. Practice seriously even after meeting the minimum WPM threshold.

General Preparation Plan — Applicable to Any Layout

Regardless of which layout your exam uses, this preparation approach builds the speed and accuracy you need:

Month 1 — Foundation

Learn All Keys

  • Week 1: Home row — both hands
  • Week 2: All vowels and matras
  • Week 3-4: All consonants
  • 20-30 minutes daily
  • Target: 10-15 WPM
Month 2 — Word Building

Words & Sentences

  • Common Odia words
  • Full sentences
  • Conjuncts and special characters
  • 30 minutes daily
  • Target: 18-25 WPM
Month 3 — Speed

Exam Ready

  • Full paragraph practice
  • Timed 10 and 25-minute sessions
  • Focus on accuracy — errors cost marks
  • 30-45 minutes daily
  • Target: 25+ WPM with 90%+ accuracy

Universal Exam Preparation Tips

  • Accuracy matters more than raw speed. In OSSC exams the typing test is marks-based — every error reduces your final score. Type clean rather than fast.
  • Practice the exact test duration. If your exam is 25 minutes of Odia, practise 25-minute continuous sessions — not 5-minute drills. Stamina matters.
  • Learn the error counting rules. Each exam has different error deduction formulas. Read the notification carefully to understand how mistakes are penalised.
  • Use the correct software. The exam centre will have specific typing software. If possible, practise on similar software before the exam day.
  • Aim 5-10 WPM above the minimum. Stress on exam day can slow you by 3-5 WPM. If the minimum is 20 WPM, target 28+ in practice.
  • Never look at the keyboard. Touch typing is essential. Candidates who look at keys cannot reach exam-level speed consistently.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Odia typing speed required for OSSC Junior Grade Typist?
As per the OSSC notification (Advt. 1744/2025), the Junior Grade Typist post requires HSC qualification and a typing certificate from a recognised institution. The Typist-cum-Scribe Assistant post explicitly requires 40 WPM in English and 20 WPM in Odia. Requirements can change with each recruitment cycle — always download and read the current official notification from ossc.gov.in.
Which keyboard layout is used in OSSC Odia typing test?
Based on the OSSC 2024-25 typing test rules, the exam uses the Odia Inscript keyboard layout. This is different from the Akruti English Phonetic layout taught on this website. Always check your specific notification — different posts may specify different layouts. If your exam uses Inscript, you will need to practise on Inscript-specific tools in addition to building your general typing speed here.
How long is the OSSC Odia typing test?
Based on the OSSC Junior Grade Typist 2024-25 typing test: English — 10 minutes (minimum 370 words) and Odia — 25 minutes (minimum 400 words). The typing test carries 50 marks added to the final merit list. This varies by post — verify from your specific notification.
What is the OSSSC typing requirement for Junior Assistant / Clerk?
Recent OSSSC notifications have required 20 WPM in Odia and 40 WPM in English for Junior Assistant and Clerk posts. This is not the same as the popular "30 WPM" figure cited on many websites — that 30 WPM figure applies to central government SSC CHSL posts, not Odisha state posts. Always verify from the current OSSSC notification at osssc.gov.in.
Is Akruti typing practice useful even if the exam uses Inscript?
Yes — for building core skills. Practising Akruti typing builds your finger muscle memory, touch typing habit, and Odia character recognition. These skills transfer to any layout. However, in the weeks before your actual exam, you must also practise specifically on the Inscript layout that your exam will use. Think of Akruti practice as building your fitness; Inscript practice as sport-specific training.
